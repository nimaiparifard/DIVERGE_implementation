---------------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT THE TWO REVIEWER COMMENTS ACTUALLY ASK
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Comment 1: "The relationship between initialization diversity and
representation diversity is not sufficiently justified."
  -> Question of MECHANISM. Show the chain:
       different init -> different adapter weights -> different embeddings
       -> different GNN predictions.

Comment 2: "Explain why different LoRA initializations necessarily produce
meaningful and complementary semantic views rather than merely random
training variance."
  This comment contains three separate questions:
  (a) CONTROL: is the diversity between inits larger than the diversity you
      get by retraining the same init with another random seed?
  (b) MEANINGFUL / SYSTEMATIC: does each init have a reproducible "character"
      (same errors, same class strengths across seeds), or are the
      differences random?
  (c) COMPLEMENTARY: does the diversity help, i.e. does combining the views
      fix errors and increase accuracy?

  About the word "necessarily": no experiment can show that inits
  *necessarily* give complementary views. Do not claim that in the reply.
  Answer with (i) a mechanism argument for why they CAN differ and
  (ii) empirical evidence of how much they differ in practice.

The key point: the old evidence in the paper (t-SNE figure, one CKA matrix,
ensemble gain) cannot answer 2(a). Those results come from ONE seed, so the
reviewer can always say "retraining with another seed would give the same
amount of diversity". Only a design with several seeds per init (experiments
1-5) can separate "init effect" from "seed noise".

1. SHARED DESIGN OF EXPERIMENTS 1-5 (experiments/diversity_common.py)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 The pool
  For one (dataset, LLM), every combination (init, LoRA seed) is one pool
  member. Example: 5 inits x 4 seeds = 20 members, each with its own trained
  LoRA adapter and its own cached node embedding.

  - Embeddings are loaded only from seed-tagged cache files
    (..._init-<init>_pool-mean_seed<seed>...pt). There is NO fallback to an
    untagged cache. A fallback would put the same embedding under several
    seeds and produce fake zero seed variance. Missing members are skipped
    and listed in the report (this is why ORTHOGONAL is absent).
  - One GNN is trained per member with a FIXED GNN seed and FIXED split
    (--gnn_seed 42). So the only differences between two members are the
    LoRA init and/or the LoRA seed. The GNN's class probabilities,
    predictions and penultimate (pre-logit) embeddings are cached in
    results/diversity/gnn_pool/ and reused by all five experiments.

  What the LoRA seed changes: classification-head init, data order, dropout
  masks, and, for random inits (Gaussian, Orthogonal) the random A matrix.
  EVA is data-driven (SVD of activations on minibatches), so the seed also
  changes the EVA init through the batches it sees. PiSSA and LoftQ are
  deterministic given the base weights: the seed does not change their
  adapter init.

1.2 The three pair types (function pair_groups)
  For each pair of members (i, j):
    seed-only : same init,      different seed  -> pure seed / training noise
    init-only : different init, same seed       -> pure initialization effect
                (same seed = same head init, data order, dropout)
    both      : different init, different seed
  In the 16-member Cora pool: 24 seed-only, 24 init-only, 72 both pairs.

  All diversity matrices are written as DISTANCES (larger = more diverse):
  1 - CKA, prediction disagreement, 1 - Jaccard, 1 - subspace overlap.

1.3 The statistical tests (reused in every experiment)
  (T1) "Init effect exists" - permutation test.
       Statistic: mean(both) - mean(seed-only). Both groups differ in seed,
       so seed noise cancels; the extra distance in "both" comes from the
       init. p-value: shuffle init labels within each seed 2000 times.
       Question: does changing the init ADD diversity on top of seed noise?
  (T2) "Init-only / seed-only ratio" + one-sided Mann-Whitney U.
       Ratio > 1 means changing only the init moves the representation MORE
       than changing only the seed. This is the direct answer to 2(a).
       (Approximate p-value: pairs that share a member are not independent.)
  (T3) PERMANOVA (Anderson 2001) on the distance matrix, once with init as
       the factor and once with seed as the factor. R^2 = share of the total
       variance explained ("init explains X%, seed explains Y%").
  (T4) Mantel test (Spearman, permutations): correlation between two pool
       distance matrices, e.g. "pairs far apart in weight space are also
       far apart in embedding space".

  T1 and T2 answer different questions. T1 can be significant while T2 is
  not: then init adds diversity, but no more than a new seed would add.
  This is exactly what happens on Cora (see below).

2. EXPERIMENT A - experiments/cka.py
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Goal
  Measure how similar the embeddings of the five inits are, for one seed.

Procedure
  1. Load the five cached LLM embedding matrices (PiSSA, Orthogonal,
     Gaussian, LoftQ, EVA) for one (dataset, LLM, seed). All have the same
     rows (the same nodes).
  2. For each pair, compute linear CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019):
        CKA(X,Y) = ||Yc^T Xc||_F^2 / (||Xc^T Xc||_F * ||Yc^T Yc||_F)
     with Xc, Yc column-centred. It is computed in feature space (d x d),
     which is cheap even for many nodes.
  3. Report the 5x5 matrix, the mean off-diagonal CKA (one number for
     ensemble diversity), the most similar and the most diverse pair.
  4. Save a heatmap PNG and two CSVs (long format per pair + one summary row).

Why CKA
  CKA compares the similarity STRUCTURE of the node set, not coordinates.
  It is invariant to rotation and isotropic scaling of the embedding space.
  Two runs that learn the same geometry in a rotated basis get CKA = 1.
  Plain cosine between embeddings would call them different. So a low CKA
  is real geometric difference, not a basis artefact.
  1 = same geometry, 0 = unrelated geometry.

Results (Cora, LLaMA-3.2-1B, 4 seeds, results/cka/cka_summary_results.csv)
  mean off-diagonal CKA: 0.864 / 0.865 / 0.868 / 0.871  (seeds 42-45)
  most similar pair   : LoftQ-EVA          CKA 0.948-0.970  (all 4 seeds)
  most diverse pair   : Orthogonal-Gaussian CKA 0.745-0.758  (all 4 seeds)

What it shows
  - The inits do not give identical embeddings (CKA clearly below 1).
  - The SAME pairs are the most/least similar in every seed. The similarity
    pattern between inits is reproducible across seeds. This is weak
    evidence against "pure noise".

What it does NOT show
  It uses one seed at a time, so it has no seed-noise baseline. CKA 0.86
  between inits does not say whether two seeds of the same init would also
  give 0.86. This is exactly what the reviewer asks. Experiment 1 adds this
  baseline.

Use in the paper
  The heatmap is a good descriptive figure (appendix, or next to the t-SNE
  figure). It must not be the only evidence.

3. EXPERIMENT B - experiments/embedding_disagreement_for_gnn_with_dffirent_intilization.py
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Goal
  Measure diversity at the level that matters for the ensemble: the GNN
  predictions.

Procedure
  1. Load the five init embeddings for one (dataset, LLM, seed).
  2. Train one GNN on each (gnn_train_and_report, same seed/split).
  3. Predict a label for every node with each GNN.
  4. For every pair: disagreement rate = share of nodes where the two GNNs
     predict different labels (on test nodes and on all nodes).
  5. Save one CSV row per pair and a 5x5 disagreement-matrix CSV.

What it shows
  Whether the representation differences survive the GNN and change
  predictions. CKA can be low while predictions are identical; then the
  diversity is useless for the ensemble.

Limits
  - Same problem as Experiment A: one seed, no seed-noise baseline.
  - Disagreement alone is not complementarity. A weak model disagrees a lot
    because it is wrong. Experiment 3 measures whether disagreement is
    useful.
  - There are no saved results yet (results/disagreement/ does not exist).
    Experiment 1 already computes the same disagreement for the full pool,
    so this script is not required anymore. It is now a special case of
    Experiment 1.

4. EXPERIMENT 1 - seed_vs_init_diversity.py
   "Is the diversity between inits larger than seed noise?"
   Answers: Comment 2(a) directly; also Comment 1.
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Procedure
  For the whole pool, compute three distance matrices:
    L1  LLM embeddings  : 1 - linear CKA between cached LoRA embeddings
    L2  GNN embeddings  : 1 - linear CKA between GNN penultimate layers
    L3  GNN predictions : label disagreement on test nodes
  For each matrix: tests T1, T2, T3, the within-init seed noise per init, and
  a heatmap sorted by init. If init dominates seed, the heatmap shows clear
  blocks along the diagonal (low distance inside each init block).

Results (Cora, 16 members)

  Level             seed-only  init-only  both    T1 p     ratio  T2 p   PERMANOVA R2
                                                                         init / seed
  LLM emb (1-CKA)    0.099      0.092     0.122   0.0005   0.93x  0.55   0.35* / 0.43*
  GNN emb (1-CKA)    0.191      0.183     0.219   0.030    0.96x  0.59   0.33  / 0.39
  Predictions        0.093      0.089     0.096   0.045    0.95x  0.90   0.21  / 0.29*
  (* = PERMANOVA p < 0.001)

  Seed noise per init (LLM, mean 1-CKA between seeds of the same init):
    LoftQ 0.064 < EVA 0.079 < PiSSA 0.098 < Gaussian 0.155

How to read it
  + T1 is significant at all three levels. Changing the init ADDS diversity
    on top of seed noise ("both" pairs are the most diverse). So init is a
    real source of diversity, not zero.
  - T2 ratio is about 0.93-0.96, NOT above 1. Changing only the init moves
    the embedding about as much as changing only the seed (slightly less).
  - PERMANOVA: seed explains more variance than init (0.43 vs 0.35 for LLM
    embeddings). Two DIFFERENT inits trained with the SAME seed (same head
    init, data order, dropout) are more similar than the SAME init trained
    with two seeds. The training trajectory shapes the representation at
    least as much as the adapter init.
  - Inits differ in stability: deterministic inits (LoftQ, PiSSA) have low
    seed noise; Gaussian has the highest (the seed re-draws its A matrix).

Honest conclusion for Cora
  Init diversity is real and additive, but it is of the SAME ORDER as seed
  diversity, not larger. This result does not support a claim that
  initialization gives "more meaningful" diversity than random variance.

5. EXPERIMENT 2 - error_consistency_analysis.py
   "Are the differences between inits systematic or random?"
   Answers: Comment 2(b).
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Idea
  If an init gives a genuinely different semantic view, its errors should be
  reproducible: the same init fails on the same nodes/classes across seeds,
  and another init fails on different nodes/classes. Pure noise gives no
  such structure.

Procedure (test nodes, balanced 4 inits x 4 seeds)
  a) Error-set distance: 1 - Jaccard(errors_i, errors_j), tests T1/T2.
  b) Error persistence per init: of the nodes an init gets wrong in at least
     one seed, the share it gets wrong in EVERY seed.
  c) Systematic rescues: nodes that init A gets wrong in every seed AND init
     B gets right in every seed. This is the strictest definition of "B
     systematically complements A".
  d) Two-way ANOVA on per-class accuracy: factors init x class, seeds are
     the replicates. The residual term is exactly the seed noise. A
     significant init x class interaction = the inits have class-specific
     strengths larger than seed noise. This also tests the paper's
     motivation for class-level ensemble weights.

Results (Cora)
  a) error distance: seed-only 0.462, init-only 0.438, both 0.473;
     T1 p = 0.062, ratio 0.95x (p = 0.95)  -> not significant
  b) persistence: PiSSA 0.30, Gaussian 0.25, LoftQ 0.38, EVA 0.35
  c) rescue matrix: 0-2 nodes per pair (out of ~540 test nodes)
     202 nodes are persistently wrong for at least one init, 75 for every
     init. The other 127 are not rescued systematically: another init gets
     them right only in SOME seeds.
  d) ANOVA: init F(3,84)=0.08, p=0.97
            class F(6,84)=45.0, p<1e-23
            init x class F(18,84)=1.48, p=0.12, partial eta2=0.24
     Per-class winners exist (e.g. Gaussian best on class 4, LoftQ on
     class 0, PiSSA on class 6), but the best init wins only 1-3 of 4
     seeds.

How to read it
  On Cora the errors of different inits are not more different than the
  errors of different seeds. There are almost no systematic rescues. The
  class-specific pattern points in the right direction (eta2 = 0.24) but is
  not significant with 4 seeds and 7 classes.

Honest conclusion for Cora
  No clear evidence of systematic, init-specific complementarity. The
  init x class trend is the most promising part. It may become significant
  on datasets with more classes (arXiv: 40, WikiCS: 10) or with more seeds.

6. EXPERIMENT 3 - complementarity_analysis.py
   "Does the diversity turn into ensemble gain?"
   Answers: Comment 2(c).
---------------------------------------------------------------------

All ensembles here are UNIFORM probability averages: no weights are
learned, nothing is fitted on test nodes. This isolates the value of
diversity from the value of the learned ensemble head.

Procedure
  a) Pairwise complementarity (Kuncheva & Whitaker, 2003) on test nodes:
     double-fault rate (both wrong), oracle accuracy (at least one right),
     oracle gain over the better member, Yule's Q. Tests T1/T2.
  b) Seed-only ensembles (1 init, k seeds) vs multi-init ensembles (k inits,
     1 seed) at the SAME size k = 4. For each, the ambiguity decomposition
     (Krogh & Vedelsby, 1995), an exact identity for a uniform average:
         ensemble error = average member error - ambiguity (diversity)
     This links diversity to gain mathematically.
  c) Diversity vs gain: every k-subset of the pool (k = 2..5), mean pairwise
     disagreement vs ensemble gain, Spearman correlation, gain grouped by
     the number of distinct inits in the subset.

Results (Cora)
  a) oracle gain seed-only 0.033, init-only 0.032 (ratio 0.96x, n.s.);
     double fault and Q also about 1.0x.
  b) k = 4:           ens acc  gain/mean  gain/best  ambiguity
     seed-only        88.76    +1.54      +0.62      0.0344
     multi-init       88.76    +1.54      +0.79      0.0320
     Multi-init > seed-only: p = 0.67 (not significant).
  c) Spearman(diversity, gain) = 0.73-0.76 for every k (p < 1e-20).
     k = 4: 1 distinct init +1.54 pp; 4 distinct inits +1.73 pp.
     k = 5: +1.84 pp for 2, 3 and 4 distinct inits (flat).

How to read it
  + Diversity is useful: more disagreement -> more ensemble gain, strongly
    and consistently (rho ~0.75). Every ensemble beats its best member.
    So the diversity is complementary, not just errors.
  - But at equal size, an ensemble of 4 inits is as good as an ensemble of 4
    seeds of one init. The source of diversity (init vs seed) does not
    matter much on Cora. Mixing inits gives at most +0.2 pp more.

Honest conclusion for Cora
  "Diversity is complementary and helps" is well supported.
  "Initialization diversity is better than seed diversity" is not.

7. EXPERIMENT 4 - lora_subspace_analysis.py
   "Mechanism: how does init diversity become representation diversity?"
   Answers: Comment 1 directly. This is the strongest experiment.
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Procedure (on the trained adapter files in artifacts/)
  a) Adapter subspace distance between members. For each of the 64 LoRA
     layers (r = 8), the adapter reads from row(A) and writes into col(B).
     Overlap of two r-dim subspaces = ||Qi^T Qj||_F^2 / r (1 = same
     subspace, r/d = random). Also cosine between the updates B_i A_i and
     B_j A_j. Tests T1/T2.
  b) Spectral position: overlap of col(B) / row(A) with the top-r and
     bottom-r singular vectors of the pretrained weight W (random = r/d).
     PiSSA starts exactly on the top-r subspace, so its overlap after
     training shows how much of the init survives training.
  c) Stage chain: Mantel tests between the distance matrices of consecutive
     stages: adapter weights -> LLM embeddings -> GNN embeddings ->
     predictions.

  Caveat (also in the code): PiSSA and LoftQ replace W with a residual, so
  B A is not the update relative to the original W. The analysis compares
  the adapter factors (the subspace the adapter reads/writes), not W_eff - W.

Results (Cora)
  a)                  seed-only  init-only  ratio  T2 p     T1 p
     A subspace        0.53       0.995     1.87x  0.013    0.0005
     B subspace        0.49       0.964     1.96x  0.00003  0.0005
     B A product       0.53       0.996     1.89x  0.002    0.0005
     seed-only distance per init (B A product):
       PiSSA 0.03, LoftQ 0.08, EVA 0.99, Gaussian 1.00
  b) overlap with pretrained singular subspaces (random: B 0.0098, A 0.0039)
                 B_top   B_bottom  A_top   A_bottom
     PiSSA       0.990   0.000     0.979   0.000   (~100x random, top)
     LoftQ       0.063   0.004     0.007   0.004   (~6x random, top)
     Gaussian    0.012   0.024     0.004   0.004   (~random, slight bottom)
     EVA         0.012   0.027     0.005   0.004   (~random, slight bottom)
     Std over seeds is ~0.000: each init's spectral position is fixed.
  c) Mantel (Spearman rho):
     adapter product -> LLM embedding     +0.76  p < 0.001
     adapter B       -> LLM embedding     +0.28  p < 0.001
     LLM embedding   -> GNN embedding     +0.55  p = 0.003
     LLM embedding   -> predictions       +0.44  p = 0.010
     GNN embedding   -> predictions       +0.25  p = 0.11 (n.s.)
     adapter B       -> predictions       +0.14  p = 0.051

How to read it
  + In weight space the init effect is strong and significant: different
    inits end in almost orthogonal adapter subspaces (distance ~1), and
    they stay there after training.
  + Each init has a distinct, reproducible spectral "address":
    PiSSA adapts the dominant singular directions of W; LoftQ weakly the
    dominant ones; Gaussian and EVA are near-random with a small tilt to the
    minor directions. This is a concrete, mechanistic reason why different
    inits can encode different information: they modify different parts of
    the pretrained weight spectrum.
  + The chain holds from weights to embeddings (rho 0.76) and from
    embeddings to predictions (rho 0.44). Members far apart in weight space
    are far apart in representation space. This is the "relationship"
    Comment 1 asks for.
  - Seed-only distance is large for Gaussian and EVA, because for these the
    seed IS part of the init (random A / data-driven SVD). For
    deterministic inits (PiSSA, LoftQ) the seed barely moves the adapter.
  - The chain weakens after the GNN: the GNN (message passing over the same
    graph) pulls the views toward each other. Weight-space diversity is
    near 1.0, but prediction disagreement is only ~9%.

Honest conclusion for Cora
  Strong evidence for the mechanism (Comment 1). But the huge weight-space
  diversity is compressed into modest representation and prediction
  diversity, and at those levels it is similar in size to seed diversity.

8. EXPERIMENT 5 - knn_neighborhood_analysis.py
   "Do inits arrange the LOCAL semantic neighbourhoods differently?"
   Answers: Comment 2 ("semantic views"); complements the global CKA.
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Idea
  CKA is global. Two views can have similar global geometry but different
  nearest neighbours for each node. A different "semantic view" means that
  a node's closest documents change.

Procedure (k = 10, cosine, for LLM and GNN embeddings)
  a) k-NN overlap distance: 1 - mean over nodes of Jaccard(kNN_A(v),
     kNN_B(v)). Tests T1/T2.
  b) k-NN label homophily per member: share of a node's 10 neighbours with
     its own label (how well a view groups same-class nodes).
  c) Neighbourhood complementarity: share of test nodes whose k-NN majority
     label is correct in at least one of g views, for g inits (same seed)
     vs g seeds (same init).

Results (Cora)
  a) LLM: seed-only 0.61, init-only 0.62, both 0.66; T1 p = 0.0005, ratio 1.02x
     GNN: seed-only 0.79, init-only 0.78, both 0.81; T1 p = 0.0005, ratio 0.99x
  b) LLM homophily: Gaussian 0.828 > PiSSA 0.810 > EVA 0.805 > LoftQ 0.800
     (std over seeds ~0.006, so the Gaussian advantage is reproducible)
  c) LLM: single view 0.817; any of 4 inits 0.886; any of 4 seeds 0.896
     GNN: single view 0.869; any of 4 inits 0.919; any of 4 seeds 0.922

How to read it
  + Neighbourhoods change a lot: about 60% of each node's 10 nearest
    neighbours differ between two views. Changing the init adds significant
    local change on top of the seed (T1).
  + Combining views gives more nodes a correct neighbourhood (+5 to +8 pp).
  - Again, init and seed produce the same amount of local change, and
    combining seeds is slightly better than combining inits.

9. OVERALL VERDICT: HOW FAR DO THE RESULTS ANSWER THE REVIEWERS?
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary of the evidence (Cora):

  Claim                                              Evidence              Verdict
  -------------------------------------------------  --------------------  ---------------
  Different inits -> different adapter subspaces     Exp 4a: ratio ~1.9x,  STRONG support
                                                     p < 0.02
  Each init has a distinct spectral "address"        Exp 4b                STRONG support
  Weight differences propagate to embeddings and     Exp 4c Mantel         Supported
  predictions                                        (0.76, 0.44)
  Init adds diversity on top of seed noise           Exp 1/5 T1,           Supported
                                                     p < 0.05
  The similarity pattern between inits is stable     Exp A                 Supported
  Diversity is complementary (turns into gain)       Exp 3c rho ~0.75      Supported
  Init diversity > seed diversity                    Exp 1,3,5 ratio ~0.95 NOT supported
  Init-specific, systematic errors/class strengths   Exp 2                 NOT supported
                                                                           (trend only)
  Multi-init ensemble > seed-only ensemble           Exp 3b p = 0.67       NOT supported

How much this answers each comment (my estimate, for Cora results as they
are now):

  Comment 1 (relationship init -> representation):  mostly answered (~75%).
    Exp 4 gives a mechanism (spectral position), a weight-space effect, and a
    Mantel chain into embeddings and predictions. This is much more than
    most papers give. The missing part: more datasets/LLMs, and the chain
    weakens at the GNN stage.

  Comment 2 (meaningful and complementary vs random variance):
    partially answered (~40-50%), IF you present it honestly.
    - "Complementary": yes (Exp 3c).
    - "Not merely random variance": only the weak form (init adds diversity
      beyond seed, T1). The strong form (init diversity is larger or more
      systematic than seed diversity) is contradicted on Cora.
    - "Necessarily": cannot be shown by any experiment; say so.

  IMPORTANT RISK: if the reply claims "init diversity is significantly
  larger than seed noise", the reviewer can check the new table and see
  ratios of 0.93-0.96 and PERMANOVA seed R2 > init R2. That would hurt
  credibility more than an honest, nuanced answer. IPM reviewers usually
  accept "we tested it; here is what holds and what does not; we changed
  the claim accordingly" if the core method still works (it does: all
  ensembles beat their members, and DIVERGE's accuracy results are
  unaffected).

  Also remember: these are single-dataset results (Cora is the smallest and
  easiest benchmark, where all views are already ~87% accurate and have
  little room to differ). The picture may be different on arXiv, PubMed or
  WikiCS. Do not generalise from Cora in either direction before running
  the other datasets.

10.3 A positive framing that the data supports
  - Initialization is a CONTROLLABLE, structured lever: each strategy
    targets a known part of the weight spectrum (top singular directions
    for PiSSA, near-random for Gaussian/EVA). Seeds are an uncontrolled
    lever. DIVERGE uses the controlled one, and the two can be combined:
    "both" pairs are the most diverse at every level (Exp 1, Exp 5).
  - With deterministic inits (PiSSA, LoftQ) the seed barely changes the
    adapter (seed-only distance 0.03 / 0.08), but changing the init moves
    it to an almost orthogonal subspace. For these strategies, the init is
    the main source of weight-space diversity.
  - The practical claim DIVERGE needs is only that the views are diverse
    AND complementary. That holds. Whether the same gain could also be
    obtained from seeds is a fair limitation to state (see Section 11).

11. DRAFT RESPONSE TO THE REVIEWERS (adapt after all datasets are run)

Comment 1 (relationship between initialization and representation diversity)

  We thank the reviewer for this comment. We added a mechanistic analysis
  (Section X, Appendix Y) that follows the effect of the initialization
  through each stage of the pipeline. (i) In weight space, the trained
  adapters of different initializations occupy nearly orthogonal subspaces
  (subspace distance 0.96-0.99 vs 0.49-0.53 between seeds of the same
  initialization; permutation p < 0.001). (ii) Each initialization keeps a
  distinct and seed-stable position in the spectrum of the pretrained
  weights: PiSSA adapters remain in the top-r singular subspace (overlap
  0.99, about 100x the random baseline), LoftQ weakly favours the dominant
  directions, and Gaussian/EVA adapters are close to random with a slight
  tilt toward minor directions. Each strategy therefore modifies a
  different part of the pretrained representation. (iii) Mantel tests show
  that these weight-space differences propagate: adapter distance
  correlates with LLM-embedding distance (rho = 0.76, p < 0.001), and
  embedding distance correlates with GNN prediction disagreement
  (rho = 0.44, p = 0.01).

Comment 2 (meaningful and complementary views vs random training variance)

  We agree that this required a controlled test. We trained every
  initialization with four LoRA seeds and compared pairs that differ only
  in the seed with pairs that differ only in the initialization, at the
  level of LLM embeddings (CKA), GNN embeddings, predictions, error sets
  and k-NN neighbourhoods. Changing the initialization adds significant
  diversity on top of seed variance at every level (permutation tests,
  p <= 0.045). At the representation and prediction level, however, the
  size of the initialization effect is comparable to the seed effect
  (ratio 0.93-0.96). We therefore do not claim that initialization
  necessarily produces more meaningful diversity than random training
  variance. What the new experiments support is: (a) initializations
  provide a controllable and structured source of diversity (distinct
  spectral subspaces, stable across seeds); (b) the resulting diversity is
  complementary: ensemble gain increases monotonically with member
  disagreement (Spearman rho = 0.75, p < 1e-20), and every ensemble
  exceeds its best member; (c) initialization and seed diversity are
  additive, since members that differ in both are the most diverse. We
  revised the contribution statement and added this analysis and its
  limitations to Sections X and Z.

  [If other datasets show ratio > 1 or significant init x class
   interaction, report that here as the stronger result.]
